01

The problem

For the pitch, the video, and anyone non-technical. This is the whole argument.

Two numbers make the case. A campus runs hundreds of cameras against a handful of officers, so most of it is unwatched most of the time. And the International Association of Chiefs of Police puts up to 98% of US security alarm activations as false, which costs 36 million police calls a year and 10 to 20% of patrol officers' time. So the existing systems that do alert are mostly crying wolf, and operators stop trusting them.

Our target user is the campus security officer. Sentinel gives them a system that watches everything they cannot, stays quiet unless it is sure, and acts on its own when waiting would cost time that matters.

02

Hard rules and scoring

From the organizers' student guide and the judging criteria. Breaking any of these disqualifies or costs points.

MUST

All AI inference runs on the event's ZGX Nano. Not a laptop, not a cloud GPU.

NEVER

No cloud AI APIs for reasoning. No OpenAI, Gemini, Anthropic, SageMaker, Vertex, Azure ML in the runtime path. Frontier models for planning and for vibe-coding the web pages are explicitly allowed, and we say so openly.

MUST

Models are vLLM-compatible. Z Runtime wraps vLLM. If it does not serve under vLLM, it is not in the system.

MUST

Serve through HP Z Runtime, not raw Ollama. HP integration is 25 of 100 points, the largest bucket, and a judge on every panel knows the difference on sight.

MUST

One explicit, defensible, measurable escalation decision. Ours: all understanding is local, one calibrated threshold decides whether we reach outside the building, and what crosses is a voice and a text brief, never a frame.

NEVER

On a call, never infer a fact. Addresses, coordinates, building names and medical status are read from the Call Brief and camera map. If it is not in the brief, the system says it cannot confirm it.

NEVER

Never dial a real emergency number. Calls go only to a verified teammate's phone, and everything is labelled simulated in the UI, the README and the video.

NEVER

Never let SignalWire do speech. No <Say>, no <Gather input="speech">. That would be cloud AI inference. We use a bidirectional media stream: SignalWire carries raw audio, our models do all the speech work on the Nano.

MUST

Everything lives in the repo. The Nano is wiped at the end of the event.

NEVER

Do not SSH into another team's device, saturate shared egress, or reboot and walk away. A reboot kills Tailscale and needs someone physically in the lab.

03

How it works, plain

The whole system in one paragraph, then one sentence.

Camera feeds arrive on the Nano. A cheap watcher screens every frame and throws almost all of them away for near-zero cost. The handful that look interesting get handed to a large vision model that actually looks and judges what is happening, how sure it is, where it is, and what the person looks like. Code, not a model, compares that confidence to a measured threshold and decides what happens: minor goes to campus security as a message for them to judge, severe places a call immediately and broadcasts to security at the same moment. Once a call is live, the system speaks for itself and answers the dispatcher's questions from facts it already holds. Meanwhile it follows the person from camera to camera and keeps the security timeline updated. Nothing that could identify a student ever leaves the building.

In one sentence: a cheap guard watches everything and discards almost all of it, so that when something real happens an expensive expert can afford to look properly, and a human is only interrupted when the machine admits it is unsure.

04

The four tiers

All locked. Each box lists what runs, in order, and why the tier exists.

C · ROUTER  —  milliseconds, runs on everything

  1. Frame scheduler. Motion decides how often we look, never whether we look. Any person already being tracked is sampled regardless of motion.
  2. YOLO26s-pose, TensorRT, batched across cameras. One model giving boxes, 17 keypoints and confidence.
  3. ByteTrack. Turns boxes into continuous people, gives each one a direction of travel.
  4. Rolling per-track state, the last 1 to 2 seconds of box, keypoints, torso angle and vertical velocity.
  5. Temporal rules. A fall is a sequence: upright, fast descent, orientation flip, stays low. Also running, sudden acceleration, long dwell.
  6. VadCLIP, about 1 fps per tracked person. Catches what posture cannot: theft, fighting at distance.
  7. Fusion score. Fifteen lines of weighted sum, one threshold.
  8. Escalate the last 8 seconds from the ring buffer.
Why it existsTuned loose on purpose. A wrong escalation costs seconds of GPU and disturbs nobody. A missed incident is invisible forever. Miss nothing, let the expensive model do the rejecting.

D · ADJUDICATE  —  seconds, runs rarely

  1. Event-aware sampling. 16 frames from the 8-second window, weighted toward the router's peak timestamp rather than spread evenly.
  2. HP Z Runtime on the Nano, OpenAI-compatible endpoint on :8000. Not replaced by bare vLLM.
  3. Qwen3-VL-30B-A3B-Instruct-FP8, the official Qwen checkpoint. NVFP4 tested day one as an upgrade if it loads clean.
  4. Prefill once with the frames plus factual metadata only: track_id, camera_id, peak timestamp, target person. Never the router's scores.
  5. Completion A: a single token from the six-class set. Read the logprobs, temperature-calibrate them.
  6. Completion B: constrained JSON for description and location, reusing the cached prefix.
  7. Fuse the VLM probability with the router's score in code. This is the number the dispatch threshold reads.
  8. Deduplicate on track_id plus time window, then emit or update the IncidentRecord.
Why independence mattersIf we hand the VLM the router's verdict it will agree with the router, which defeats having two tiers. And since we fuse both scores afterwards, telling one about the other double-counts the same evidence and quietly breaks the calibration.

E · ACT  —  deterministic, no model in the decision

  1. Entry: IncidentRecord, fused calibrated probability, incident_id.
  2. Thresholds, plain code, three outcomes.
  3. State machine (transitions): NEW → ALERTED → DISPATCH_PENDING → DISPATCHED → TRACKING → RESOLVED, plus DISMISSED.
  4. Idempotency: every action keyed on incident_id plus action name. A retry never fires twice.
  5. Guardrails: one dispatch per incident ever, an hourly cap, a global kill switch on the dashboard.
  6. Security notified always, with the clip and the reasoning.
  7. Incident timeline, appended to by every event, rendered live.
  8. Call Brief assembled from record plus camera map plus tracking state. Lookups only.
  9. Dispatch via a SignalWire media stream to a verified teammate's phone. Browser console as the fallback.
  10. Agentic loop during the call: lookup_location, get_suspect_status, get_elapsed_time, get_person_description, repeat_last.
  11. Audit log: every action, reason, confidence, timestamp.
The line for the pitchNo language model ever decides to call for help. Code does, from a calibrated number, once, with a rate limit. The model's job starts after that decision, and it is only allowed to describe what it actually saw.

F · SURFACE  —  what the judges actually see

  1. Camera wall, six MJPEG panes with server-drawn overlays: boxes, track IDs, live router score.
  2. Campus map, twelve pins. During a chase the path draws itself and the predicted next camera lights up before the person arrives, then confirms.
  3. Incident queue, severity colour and state badge.
  4. Incident detail: the 8-second clip on loop, the model's reasoning in its own words, calibrated confidence, which rules fired.
  5. Call console: both transcripts side by side, the Call Brief visible, and tool calls appearing live, which is the proof of the agentic loop.
  6. Health strip: cameras online, models resident, GPU utilization, p95, and the running counter of frames screened and escalated.
  7. Demo control, hidden behind a key: reset to zero, scenario picker, pre-warm indicator.
Design for the quiet state99% of operation, and the first 15 seconds of our video. Six panes with boxes tracking calmly, the counter climbing, everything green, no alerts. It should look like a system working hard and finding nothing, because that is what it is.
05

Services and deployment

Five containers, one host service. Reproducibility is a scoring metric, so one command has to bring the whole thing up on any machine.

Why five services and not nine

Vision keeps decode, router and tracking together because they share GPU context and frame buffers, and splitting them means shipping frames over IPC for no benefit. Brain keeps adjudication, the state machine and the Call Brief together because they share incident state. Everything else is genuinely separate. Fewer boundaries, fewer bugs, fewer things to restart at 2am.

Deployment: Docker Compose, nothing else

One machine, one compose file. docker compose up brings up all five services with GPU passthrough. That satisfies the "script to run it on another machine" deliverable completely, it works anywhere Docker and the NVIDIA runtime exist, and it cannot fail in a way that costs us Friday.

Compose is the whole deployment story. Five services, one file, GPU passthrough, reproducible on any machine with Docker and the NVIDIA runtime. Nothing else ships.

The scale story is the shape, not another platform: five stateless services, one config file, one camera map. One box is one deployment. Another campus is another box. We say that plainly, and it is true.

# the only three commands anyone needs
make up       # docker compose up, pulls models if missing, waits for health
make demo     # pre-warm, load scenario, open the dashboard
make reset    # back to t=0, clear incidents, keep models warm

14

Answers to have ready

Everyone should be able to give these without thinking.

Cloud
  • All understanding is local. One boundary, one calibrated decision, and what crosses is a voice and a text brief, never a frame. You cannot make a phone ring from a local network without a carrier, which is exactly why SignalWire is there and why it never touches AI.
Novelty
  • NVIDIA VSS is the reference architecture for video analytics agents and we know it. We diverge on three things it does not do: a cheap ML router in front of the VLM, autonomous outbound dispatch with live dialogue, and cross-camera pursuit.
Competitors
  • Volt AI, Verkada, Rhombus and Spot AI all solve false alarms by putting humans in a cloud operations center to verify alerts. We solve it with a cascade that runs in the building. No video leaving, no per-seat monitoring cost.
Confidence
  • It is not a number the model wrote. We constrain the class to one token, read the logprobs, and temperature-scale them on a held-out slice. Here is the reliability curve.
Why 30B
  • GB10 is bandwidth bound. A Mixture-of-Experts model reads 3B of 30B per token, so it measured faster than a dense 8B while carrying far more capacity. Dense of comparable quality costs roughly ten times the bandwidth here.
Ethics
  • No identity database, no roster matching, appearance only, short retention, and nothing identifying crosses the boundary. Every action is in an audit log with its reason and its confidence. Say it on a slide before anyone asks.
Scale
  • Five stateless services, one compose file, one camera map. One box is one deployment. Another campus is another box, not a reconfiguration.
